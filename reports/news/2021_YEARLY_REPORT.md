## Executive Summary: The Year in Review

The year 2021 was defined by a **"weird" economic dichotomy**: robust equity market gains contrasted against severe supply chain disruptions and the highest inflation in four decades [1-3]. While major indexes achieved significant growth—the **S&P 500 rose approximately 27%** and the **Nasdaq 100 gained 27%**—the real economy grappled with the "Great Resignation" and persistent COVID-19 variants (Delta and Omicron) [1, 4, 5].

Key overarching catalysts and structural shifts included:
*   **Monetary & Fiscal Support:** Massive liquidity from the Federal Reserve and federal stimulus programs (relief checks and child tax credits) bolstered consumer savings and spending power [4, 6, 7].
*   **Inflation Pivot:** Inflation, initially deemed "transitory" by the Federal Reserve, surged as the Consumer Price Index (CPI) topped 5% annual increases every month starting in June [8-10].
*   **Sector Leadership Shift:** Energy (XLE) and Real Estate became the top-performing sectors, with Energy posting its best year ever, up over 40% [11, 12].
*   **Tech Trajectory:** While Big Tech drove index returns, a "Great AI Reckoning" began as researchers highlighted the diminishing returns and high computational costs of deep learning [13, 14].
*   **Labor Market Transformation:** A record 74% of Americans viewed the period as a "good time to get a job" even as 70% felt the economy was worsening, leading to unprecedented wage growth in low-wage sectors [3, 15, 16].

## Quarterly Chronology

### Q1: Transition and Speculative Fervor
*   **Defining Moments:** The inauguration of Joe Biden as the 46th President and the aftermath of the January 6th Capitol attack dominated the political landscape [17, 18].
*   **Market Reaction:** The "Meme Stock" craze saw GameStop (GME) prices rise 900 times their record low, leading to major losses for some investment funds and retail trading restrictions [19].
*   **Macro/Geopolitical:** Vaccine rollouts accelerated, with Texas becoming the first state to administer one million doses by mid-January [18]. Small-cap and value stocks initially led the market on recovery optimism [20].

### Q2: Inflationary Acceleration
*   **Defining Moments:** US inflation reached a 13-year record high during this period [21].
*   **Market Reaction:** Equity indexes continued an upward trajectory despite emerging bond market concerns as the 10-year Treasury yield fluctuated [22].
*   **Macro/Geopolitical:** G7 nations reached a historic agreement on new rules for global corporate taxation [21]. The grounding of the *Ever Given* in the Suez Canal in March caused ripple effects through global supply chains into Q2 [23].

### Q3: Supply Bottlenecks and Regulatory Shocks
*   **Defining Moments:** Global supply chain faltering became a household term as port congestion and semiconductor shortages (notably affecting Ford's vehicle sales) intensified [23].
*   **Market Reaction:** Emerging markets suffered a significant 8.09% decline [24]. However, the Nasdaq closed above 15,000 for the first time [24].
*   **Macro/Geopolitical:** The US completed its withdrawal from Afghanistan in August, resulting in the Taliban returning to power and a drop in presidential approval ratings [25, 26].

### Q4: The Omicron Surge and Fed Hawkishness
*   **Defining Moments:** The emergence of the Omicron variant in late 2021 caused temporary economic slowdowns in specific sectors [5, 15].
*   **Market Reaction:** The Federal Reserve announced a doubling of the pace for tapering net asset purchases in December, signaling an end to easy money policies by March 2022 [27].
*   **Macro/Geopolitical:** Energy prices continued to climb, on track to be 80% higher in 2021 than in 2020, further fueling global inflation [28].
