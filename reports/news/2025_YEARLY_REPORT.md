## Executive Summary: The Year in Review

2025 was a year of profound **structural economic shifts** and **geopolitical redirection**, defined primarily by the return of Donald Trump to the White House and a "sea change" in U.S. federal policy [1, 2]. The macroeconomic narrative was characterized by **fiscal tightening**, **increased trade protectionism**, and an aggressive pivot toward **fossil fuels** at the expense of green energy initiatives [1, 2].

*   **Economic Performance:** Real GDP growth slowed to an estimated **2% for the full year**, down from previous periods [3]. While the third quarter saw a robust **4.4% increase**, growth decelerated sharply to **1.4% in Q4 2025** [4, 5].
*   **Major Catalysts:** The 2025 administration launched a "slash-and-burn" approach to the federal bureaucracy and sought to implement high tariffs, which caused the Federal Reserve to pause or consider reversing past monetary easing [1, 6].
*   **Tech Trends:** Artificial Intelligence continued its dominance as a core industrial driver; by late 2025, OpenAI reported its ChatGPT ecosystem had reached **800 million weekly users** and introduced the **AgentKit** for scaling AI agents [7].
*   **Structural Shifts:** A significant **reorientation of trade flows** occurred as U.S. firms actively reduced reliance on China, shifting production to the U.S. or lower-cost regional partners like Mexico [8, 9].

## Quarterly Chronology

*   **Q1 2025: Administrative Reconfiguration**
    The year began with the inauguration of Donald Trump, marking an immediate shift toward **deregulation and fiscal consolidation** [2, 10]. The administration prioritized slashing government spending and drawing back from various overseas obligations [1, 2].
*   **Q2 2025: Tariff Implementation & Market Volatility**
    Initial pass-through effects of new tariff increases began impacting the price level, while the labor market started to show signs of cooling [3, 11]. Markets reacted to the "higher-for-longer" potential of interest rates as the Fed evaluated the inflationary impact of trade restrictions [6, 12].
*   **Q3 2025: Economic Divergence**
    U.S. real GDP surged at an annual rate of **4.4%**, with all 50 states and D.C. reporting increases [4, 13]. During this period, the U.S. current-account deficit narrowed to **$226.4 billion**, representing 2.9% of GDP [14].
*   **Q4 2025: Deceleration & AI Scaling**
    Real GDP growth dropped to **1.4%** as decreases in government spending and exports weighed on the economy [4, 5]. In December 2025, the international trade deficit widened to **$70.3 billion** [15]. Concurrently, the tech sector saw major advancements with the release of the **o3 reasoning models** and the massive expansion of the ChatGPT app ecosystem [7, 16].
